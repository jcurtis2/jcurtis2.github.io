## Abstract

Heterogeneous reactions of N₂O₅ on aerosol particles significantly influence tropospheric chemistry and ozone formation. The uptake coefficient of N₂O₅ on aerosol surfaces depends on aerosol composition, and the detailed mixing state of the aerosol population may therefore affect overall atmospheric chemistry. This study uses particle-resolved aerosol simulations to quantify how differences in mixing state between externally mixed and internally mixed aerosol populations affect heterogeneous N₂O₅ hydrolysis. We show that mixing state can substantially affect N₂O₅ uptake and resulting HOₓ and NOₓ concentrations, with implications for air quality and ozone formation.
